Chapter 4538: Chapter 4538

A flight of light crows exploded in front of the second-in-command, blinding him, while several earth crows dove into the ground beneath him. A rock spike erupted behind him, impaling the assassin when he triggered his flight spell and bolted into Vastor’s spell at breakneck speed.

"That was the last one I don’t need." Vastor panted, bleeding from several cuts all over his body. "It’s over. You’re safe now."

The Master cast a Life Sensing array and detected several powerful life forces approaching in one of the Queen’s Corps standard formations.

"Are you sure?" Ryssa asked, not daring to move from her spot.

"Check your amulet." Vastor bolted to the paralyzed assassin just in time to save him from the poison pill he had just swallowed. "Not so fast, boy. You are going to die only when you’ve outlived your usefulness and on my terms."

A wave of his hand extracted the essence of the Dream Root straight from the assassin’s bloodstream. It destroyed the nerve receptors of its victims so quickly they seemed to fall asleep.

Even if someone managed to detox someone who had ingested Dream Root before they died, the damage to their life forces would make them insensitive to pain. Like in a dream, nothing could harm them, making even the most brutal interrogation techniques useless.

"Your death won’t be quick or painless, boy." Vastor’s Body Sculpting spell, Master of Life, restored the assassin’s body to its prime. "Now talk. Who sent you here and why?"

The man replied by using his now-working dimensional ring to have many deadly pills appear in his mouth.

’Even the god of healing couldn’t stop a mix of poisons and acid. If he focuses on my flesh, my organs will rot. If he saves my organs, my flesh will melt.’ He thought, and he was right.

"This dumb trick again? You cowards have no creativity." Vastor had already conjured a layer of air in the assassin’s throat that pushed the pills out of his mouth as soon as they appeared. "What’s next? The poison needle in the boot? The self-igniting glove?"

The squad leader swallowed hard as the old man disabled his contingency measures with ease, even watching in a few places the assassin had no idea what purpose they might serve.

There were secrets of the trade even a squad leader of the Night Phantoms wasn’t aware of, yet they were old news to the Master. Not because of his status in the Queen’s Corps, but because most of Garlen’s underworld knew better than crossing him.

When the leader of the Organization asked a question, even the most fearsome group of mercenaries had only two choices. They could tell him everything he wanted to know or receive the visit of one of his Eldritches.

After a few powerful criminal organizations disappeared overnight and were replaced by their wiser, more compliant competition, everyone learned their lesson.

"Stop wasting my time, boy." Vastor lifted the man by the collar of his shirt, bringing him millimeters from his face. "You’re in the palm of my hand. Not even death can touch you unless I say so."

The Master exuded no aura or killing intent, and his equipment still stood guard at Ryssa and Dhiral. The assassin wore his best armor and his poisoned blade was in his hand, yet he couldn’t move a muscle.

From so up close, he needed sheer willpower to keep his sanity. He was akin to a fluffy bunny sitting in front of a majestic lion. Its very existence was a threat against which the assassin instinctively knew he had no defense.

Vastor’s fingers were claws that could rend the assassin’s flesh without effort, and his every word sounded like the low growl of a hungry beast. The man swallowed hard, feeling his body becoming drenched in cold sweat.

"I said talk." The Master said, and the assassin stared at his teeth in a daze as if they were rows of deadly fangs.

"My name is Alkham Sher." His voice was a quivering mess that only the terror of angering the monster in front of him made intelligible. "I’m a captain of the Night Phantoms."

"I recognized your mercenary group the moment I saw your fancy armor and ambush pattern." Vastor sneered. "Tell me something I don’t know, boy."

"The mission came in from a middleman, but it made no sense." Alkham continued. "On paper, the client was a rich merchant whose daughter’s academy application had been rejected, but we haven’t survived this long by accepting commissions without doing our due diligence first.

"The merchant had a spotless record. Someone that clean shouldn’t have the connections necessary to hire the Night Phantoms, and giving us such a high-profile mission as his first commission was an even bigger red flag.

"We followed protocol and dug into the life of the middleman and merchant to understand who the real client was."

"Smart policy." The Master nodded. "An insurance in the event the real client plans to use your organization and discard you when they don’t need you anymore, framing you for whatever crime they are trying to commit. Continue, boy."

"The investigation was long and expensive, but it was worth it." Alkham’s pupils dilated in fear until his eyes became almost completely black. "The merchant also was a middleman in a long chain of straw men that led us to Archmage Lema."

"The Headmaster of the Fire Griffon?" Vastor’s eyebrow rose, but his voice remained cold. "She didn’t like Ryssa, and opposed giving beasts and plant folk citizen rights, but it doesn’t explain why she would try to have Marth’s family murdered."

"That’s why it was worth digging her name." The assassin licked his panic-dried lips. "By completing the mission, we were going to get a mountain of gold and the power of one of the most influential Archmages of the Kingdom in our pockets."

"Answer the question, boy." The Master’s voice lowered to a whisper. "Why would she do this?"

"According to our sources, she wanted to kill Marth’s family to stop the White Griffon from becoming the First Griffon." The assassin replied.

"The First what?" Nothing Alkham said made sense, which unsettled Vastor.

’He’s telling the truth. Yet why have I not heard a single word about this either as the Master or the White Griffon’s Chief of Staff?’ He thought.

"First Griffon." Alkham repeated. "A prestigious reward and title that would mark the White Griffon as the first among equals. Your academy has produced too many Great Mages recently, and even a Magus.

"According to some rumors, Friya Ernas can even craft dimensional crystals. The Royals want to reward merit and have decided to establish the First Griffon Award. It’s like the pin the first ranked among the students receives at the end of the academic year, but for the academies."

"Make it make sense, boy." The Master snarled. "If this is true, then why do I know nothing about this?"

"I can answer that, Professor Vastor." He turned around, noticing four units of the Queen’s Corps had arrived.

The two five-man teams that originally protected Ryssa and Dhiral examined the Spirit Barrier, trying to understand what it was and reassuring the Dryad of their identity by showing their badges.

The two reinforcement units, instead, stood at attention right behind the Master.
